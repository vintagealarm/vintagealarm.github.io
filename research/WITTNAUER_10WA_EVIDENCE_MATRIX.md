# Wittnauer 10WA — Evidence Matrix & Causal Guardrails

Status: active research-control note for `research/10wa-round2`.

This file records **proof standards, causal guardrails, and source targets** for the two-track investigation:

- Track A: Wittnauer adoption / product history
- Track B: patent / CH304088A relationship

It does not replace `WITTNAUER_10WA_LEDGER.md`, `WITTNAUER_10WA_RESEARCH_DIRECTION.md`, or `WITTNAUER_10WA_CH304088_AUDIT.md`.

## 1. Guardrail: avoid the "too-clean story" trap

The current four-factor adoption model is useful because each factor can be tested independently:

1. **Module economics** — reuse an existing base movement rather than develop a complete integrated alarm calibre.
2. **One-control / two-task UI** — combine alarm setting and alarm winding in one bezel operation.
3. **Product differentiation** — distinguish the external controls / appearance from contemporary alarm watches.
4. **Market transition around AS 1475** — the alarm-watch market was beginning to include standardized mass-produced alarm ebauches.

Do not convert the coherence of this model into proof.

Especially avoid the unsupported causal chain:

`AS 1475 appeared in 1954 -> proprietary 10WA became uneconomic -> Wittnauer abandoned 10WA -> Wittnauer adopted AS`

Currently supported only at a lower level:

- 10WA and the early AS 1475 period are chronologically close.
- Wittnauer later used AS-family alarm movements.

Safe wording until Wittnauer-side primary evidence appears:

> The arrival and spread of AS 1475 may have changed the market environment in which the 10WA concept had to compete.

Prefer also:

> The mid-1950s were a transition period in which proprietary / modular alarm solutions and standardized mass-produced alarm ebauches began to coexist.

Do not write `the market's standard solution changed in 1954` unless the exact chronology is independently established.

## 2. LeCoultre / Memovox: context, not causation

Longines-Wittnauer's U.S. distribution relationship with LeCoultre is useful as **market-category context**.

It can support the limited proposition that Longines-Wittnauer was already exposed to the alarm-watch category, its customers, service expectations, and competing product forms.

It does **not** currently support:

- Memovox directly inspired 10WA,
- Wittnauer intentionally chose a bezel to differentiate from Memovox,
- engineering or technology transfer from LeCoultre,
- a comparative product strategy unless period Wittnauer material says so.

Potential upgrade condition:

A Wittnauer advertisement / dealer bulletin / comparison text explicitly promoting points such as `no extra crown`, `simple bezel setting`, `easier than conventional alarm watches`, or another contemporary comparison.

If such evidence appears, the claim may move from `market context` to `documented product strategy`.

## 3. Evidence-type matrix by causal layer

Do not ask one source class to answer every question.

| Causal layer | Highest-value evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Design goal | patent text; Wittnauer advertisement; dealer bulletin; product-introduction memo |
| Mechanism | patent; production 10WA; service sheet; parts drawing |
| Trade-off | patent; user instructions; service literature; repair evidence |
| Production implementation | parts list; module drawing; teardown; service manual |
| User consequence | user instructions; advertising; period review; dealer instruction |
| Service consequence | watchmaker bulletin; service memo; parts catalog; repair notes |
| Commercial consequence | dealer material; price list; trade press; warranty / service records |
| Successor decision | calibre chronology; catalog chronology; procurement / company bulletin; dealer / service documentation |

Rule:

> A source can strongly support one layer while saying nothing about the next layer. Never collapse multiple causal layers into one story without bridge evidence.

## 4. AS 1475 chronology must be decomposed

The phrase `AS 1475 was born in 1954` is not precise enough for causal use.

At minimum separate:

- design completion,
- patent / legal filing if applicable,
- production start,
- trade / Basel presentation,
- earliest known customer adoption,
- first documented retail availability,
- large-scale circulation / multi-brand adoption.

High-value question:

> **When was AS 1475 actually available as a realistic procurement option to a manufacturer such as Wittnauer?**

Why this matters:

- If 10WA was planned before AS 1475 was commercially available, the choice looks different.
- If both were developed in parallel, the later switch may reflect a changed market rather than an initially irrational choice.
- If Wittnauer launched 10WA after AS 1475 was already readily available, then the proprietary / modular choice becomes a more deliberate strategic puzzle.

Until these dates are separated, classify AS 1475 as `ADOPTED chronology / HOLD causal interpretation`.

## 5. Fifth factor candidate: after-sales / service economics

Do not merge this automatically into `market standardization`.

Potential factor 5:

**After-sales and parts-supply economics**.

An unusual proprietary alarm module may impose:

- dedicated spare parts,
- dedicated service instructions,
- dealer / watchmaker training,
- warranty handling,
- stocking burden,
- diagnosis complexity,
- dependence on low-volume proprietary components.

A standardized AS-family alarm movement could potentially reduce some of these burdens by fitting an established parts and service ecosystem.

Classification: `OPEN / causal candidate`.

Do not claim this as a Wittnauer motive unless supported by service / dealer / parts / warranty evidence.

Useful distinction:

- Factor 4 = procurement / manufacturing environment.
- Factor 5 = post-sale maintenance / service environment.

## 6. Status vocabulary for article-grade research

For this project, preserve unresolved material rather than smoothing it away.

Use these levels when summarizing 10WA:

- **KNOWN / ADOPTED** — directly supported and article-usable.
- **STRONG** — multiple strong sources / close structural correspondence, but one bridge may still be missing.
- **HOLD** — plausible and potentially important, but incomplete.
- **CONFLICT** — credible sources disagree; preserve both.
- **OPEN QUESTION** — unresolved and worth tracking.
- **REJECTED / OBSOLETE** — do not revive without new evidence.

The number of OPEN questions is not a weakness. For VINTAGE ALARM it is part of the research value, provided each gap is explicit and updateable.

## 7. Primary-source search order for the next rounds

For the adoption-history track, prioritize **service / technical documentation before advertising**, while original guarantee / instruction material gets its own highest-priority pass because it can bridge product, service, chronology, and patent-marking questions at once.

### Tier 0 — immediate target

- original 10WA guarantee booklet
- original instruction booklet / retailer instruction card
- warranty / service card from Ref.1215 / 1216 sets

Look specifically for:

- `PAT.` / `PATENTED`
- operating warnings
- alarm winding limit / special sequence
- service address
- corporate / distributor name
- print code / revision number
- model / reference notation

### Tier 1 — highest technical value

- Wittnauer parts catalogues
- Longines-Wittnauer service bulletins
- 10WA repair instructions / dealer service sheets
- interchange manuals that directly name `10WA`
- module / movement drawings
- warranty / service documentation

Why first:

A single line such as `10WA = base calibre XXX + alarm attachment XXX` could materially change the Longines-vs-AS conflict and clarify the product architecture.

### Tier 2 — product-intent evidence

- 1953–1957 Wittnauer advertisements
- dealer bulletins
- launch material
- retailer instruction cards
- guarantee booklets from Ref.1215 / 1216
- trade press / product announcements

These sources are better for answering:

> **What did Wittnauer think it was selling?**

Look for contemporary claims about convenience, reduced controls, bezel setting, patented construction, service requirements, and operating warnings.

## 8. Two-track integration rule

Track A and Track B must inform each other without being collapsed.

### Track A — adoption / product history

Questions:

- Why did Wittnauer choose this product architecture?
- What did it claim as the user benefit?
- How was the product serviced and sold?
- When and why did Wittnauer move to other alarm calibres?

### Track B — patent / CH304088A

Questions:

- Is there a direct legal / commercial / technical bridge to Wittnauer?
- Which CH304088A features correspond to 10WA production parts?
- Which features differ?
- Did Wittnauer modify the concept, and can that modification be documented?

Integration rule:

> Structural similarity can guide the search, but only Wittnauer-side or legal bridge evidence can turn `strong correspondence` into a documented historical relationship.

Likewise:

> A plausible market history can explain why a design would make sense, but only contemporary Wittnauer evidence can establish why Wittnauer actually chose it.

## 9. Current framing to preserve

The research target is no longer merely `a rare bezel-operated alarm watch`.

Working framing:

> Wittnauer Cal.10WA sits near a mid-1950s transition where proprietary / modular alarm solutions and standardized alarm ebauches coexisted. Its one-bezel concept is technically distinctive, but its adoption motive, exact base movement, relationship to CH304088A, production implementation choices, and discontinuation logic remain only partly resolved.

This is a research framing, not a final historical conclusion.

The remaining `why` must be answered, if possible, by Wittnauer / Longines-Wittnauer primary evidence rather than by a clean retrospective narrative.

## 10. Patent-proof discipline: dates, claims, and bridges

### Separate patent dates

Never use `1954 patent` as if it were the origin date of the idea.

For CH304088A currently verified:

- filing: `1952-12-04`
- publication: `1954-12-31`
- grant / registration / legal-effect date: `OPEN` until independently verified

Editorial consequence:

> The documented architecture existed at least by the December 1952 filing date.

### Separate CLAIM / DESCRIPTION / OPTIONAL OR DEPENDENT IMPLEMENTATION

Do not treat every sentence in the specification as equal in legal meaning.

Use `WITTNAUER_10WA_CH304088_CLAIM_CHART.md`.

Important current finding:

- the **slipping bridle appears in dependent sub-claim 6** in the checked CH304088A publication.
- therefore it is stronger than a casual descriptive suggestion, but it is still **not a requirement of every broader claimed implementation**.
- production 10WA behavior does not show the same full-wind setting freedom, but actual production bridle construction remains `OPEN`.

### Separate structural correspondence from legal / commercial connection

Even near-identity of mechanism cannot prove:

- assignment,
- licence,
- purchase,
- supply agreement,
- common designer,
- technology transfer.

Those require bridge evidence.

## 11. Competing A / B / C hypotheses

Maintain three live explanations for the CH304088A ↔ 10WA correspondence.

- **A — authorized transfer / licence / acquisition:** Bliss’s rights or concept reached Wittnauer and was modified for production.
- **B — technical contact without proven patent-rights chain:** supplier / designer / prototype / know-how contact existed, but the legal chain is indirect or undocumented.
- **C — independent convergence:** similar design constraints produced similar solutions independently.

Research rule:

> Search for evidence that can falsify or weaken A, B, or C, not only evidence that makes A feel plausible.

If no bridge is found after serious archive-level searching, the valid conclusion may remain:

> **Extremely strong structural correspondence is documented, but no legal or commercial technology-transfer bridge has been found in the sources checked.**

Do not overstate non-discovery as proof of independence.
