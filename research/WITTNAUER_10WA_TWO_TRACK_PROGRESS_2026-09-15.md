## Track B — patent / CH304088A progress

### B1. Claim-by-claim comparison shows a strong core match but not an identical embodiment

`CH304088A` is stronger than a thematic resemblance because several claimed / described elements map directly onto production 10WA evidence.

#### Strong correspondence

1. **Alarm module outside the base movement, between movement and dial**
   - Patent: explicit main claim / description.
   - 10WA: Horlbeck / Beitl and teardown evidence place the alarm module on the dial side.

2. **Alarm mechanism has its own plate**
   - Patent: subclaim 1.
   - 10WA: teardown shows a separate stacked alarm module / plate assembly attached to the base movement.

3. **Rotating bezel drives alarm winding through internal gearing**
   - Patent: description + subclaim 2.
   - 10WA: teardown photographs / description show a protruding gear engaging the inside of the bezel to wind the alarm mainspring.

4. **Same bezel action also drives alarm-time setting**
   - Patent: description + subclaim 4.
   - 10WA: documented production behavior; the alarm hand moves while the bezel winds the alarm spring.

5. **Separate gong / hammer acoustic system**
   - Patent: steel-wire gong and hammer are described.
   - 10WA: surviving Ref.1216 evidence and specialist books document a gong / tone spring and hammer associated with the bezel / case area.

These correspondences make `strong structural correspondence` a defensible classification.

### B2. Important divergences are now equally valuable

The production 10WA does not appear to be a literal one-to-one copy of every patent subclaim.

#### Bezel retention

- Patent subclaim 3 describes the rotating bezel being retained by members / screws entering a circular groove.
- A detailed 10WA overhaul report describes the production bezel as a snap-fit / press-fit component removed carefully with a case-opening tool.

Classification: `HOLD — likely implementation divergence`, because this is currently based on one detailed surviving-watch teardown and may vary by reference / case execution.

#### External index on rotating part

- Patent subclaim 5 describes the rotating bezel / glass carrying an index.
- Surviving 10WA examples, including the owned specimen, instead show a plain rotating bezel while a dedicated central alarm hand indicates the selected alarm time.

Classification: `ADOPTED as observed production difference` for the examined examples; do not generalize to every hypothetical 10WA variant without evidence.

#### Slipping bridle

- Patent subclaim 6 describes a sliding / slipping bridle so alarm setting remains free even at full wind.
- Horlbeck describes production 10WA behavior in which the bezel stops at roughly full alarm wind and forcing it risks tooth damage.
- No teardown / service source yet directly identifies the production alarm mainspring attachment as fixed bridle vs slipping bridle.

Classification: `OPEN exact construction / strong functional divergence`.

### B3. Patent relationship is therefore better framed as `core architecture match + production modifications`

Current research framing:

> CH304088A and Cal.10WA share the core architecture of a dial-side alarm module with its own energy store, driven by a rotating bezel that couples alarm winding and alarm-time setting. Surviving production evidence also shows differences in case / bezel execution and setting indication, while the slipping-bridle question remains unresolved.

This is stronger and safer than either extreme:

- too weak: `they merely look similar`, or
- too strong: `CH304088A is definitively the 10WA patent`.

The next bridge question is now sharper: **did Wittnauer licence / adopt the core Bliss concept and modify its production embodiment, or did the similarity arise through another route?**

### B4. Patent-register search status

## Round 2 — concrete archival progress

### A5. NAWCC Library has exactly the Wittnauer service material this research needs

The NAWCC Library & Research Center Technical Information Directory lists, in **Box W1**:

- Wittnauer notebook section: `Calibers: 3W to D13T, 90 & 1317 to 1557`
- Wittnauer `Mainspring Chart`
- Wittnauer `Pricelists`
- Wittnauer `Repair Bulletins`
- Wittnauer `Catalog of Interchangeable Watch Materials (1912)`

The same directory also lists period cross-reference material that can independently test the base-calibre problem:

- BestFit Swiss Material Catalogs: **1952 and 1956**
- BestFit Book 104: **1954 Systems & Assorted Charts**
- BestFit Automatic Material: **1954 and 1957**
- Swiss Watch Repair Parts: **1949 and 1955**
- Watch Material Distributors Association Catalog of Watch Parts: **1957**

Classification: `ADOPTED archival-holdings fact / HIGH-VALUE retrieval target`.

Why this is important:

- this is no longer a generic hope that a service sheet might exist; a specialist archive explicitly holds Wittnauer repair bulletins, calibre material, price lists, and period interchange catalogues;
- one of these sources could directly identify `10WA`, its base calibre, its alarm attachment, or its service warnings;
- the 1952/1954/1955/1956/1957 material is chronologically close enough to test both launch architecture and the later AS-family transition.

Do not claim that Box W1 contains a 10WA bulletin until the actual pages are inspected.

### A6. Longines-Wittnauer had a numbered technical-bulletin system aimed at watchmakers

Digitized examples confirm a formal `INFORMATION BULLETIN` series published by Longines-Wittnauer Watch Co., Inc.:

- No. 572 — `FAST TRAIN WATCHES`
- No. 574 — `SERVICING — THE LONGINES AUTOMATIC`
- No. 575 — `RESERVE POWER INDICATOR / LeCOULTRE CALIBER 481`

No. 574 explicitly directs technical inquiries to the **Technical Information Service, Longines-Wittnauer Watch Co., Inc., 580 Fifth Avenue, New York 36, N.Y.**

Classification: `ADOPTED existence of technical-bulletin channel / OPEN 10WA bulletin`.

Research consequence:

- searching only consumer advertising is insufficient;
- the most likely contemporary source for special 10WA servicing / operating cautions may be this technical-information channel or the NAWCC `Repair Bulletins` holdings;
- bulletin numbers must not be assumed to map cleanly to calendar chronology without dated originals.

### A7. A generic Wittnauer guarantee-and-instructions booklet with 1948 copyright survives in the market

Current marketplace indexing shows a `Wittnauer Guarantee And Instructions Booklet: Copyright 1948` and other generic Longines-Wittnauer guarantee/instruction booklets.

Classification: `HOLD / format-and-revision lead`, **not 10WA-specific evidence**.

Do not attach the 1948 booklet to any 10WA set without a matching surviving-paper image.
