# Modern De Luxe — promotional-goods / distribution research — 2026-09-30

## Purpose
This log follows the audited Socratic decomposition of the Modern De Luxe problem. It does not attempt to infer a clock OEM from jewel count or case similarity. The target is the commercial chain: trademark/user → product planner → manufacturer → exporter/wholesaler → promotional-goods supplier → corporate customer.

## Evidence grades
- ADOPTED = physical/listing evidence supports existence/marking/use, without silently upgrading seller dating or maker attribution.
- HOLD = useful lead or working model lacking a direct corporate bridge.
- REJECTED = not usable for the claimed inference without new evidence.
- OPEN = unresolved.

## 3. ADOPTED — cross-category name identity is now stronger than a generic `Modern` resemblance
Clock corpus already establishes `Modern De Luxe` / `Modern de Luxe` on Japanese mechanical travel alarms, including Japan-attributed 2-jewel examples. The lighter corpus above independently uses the same `Modern De Luxe` wording.

Examples:
- https://www.ebay.com/itm/205293449679
- https://www.label-emmaus.co/fr/reveil-de-voyage-modern-de-luxe-japan-109797152/

This is materially stronger than merely observing that Hirota made `Modern Feudor` or that H.M.C. lighters use `MODERN`: the exact `Modern De Luxe` designation now spans at least clocks and lighters.

It still does **not** prove a common legal owner, common manufacturer, Hirota manufacture, or H.M.C. identity.

## 4. HOLD — promotional-goods / distribution-brand model
The combined evidence supports testing this model:

`Modern De Luxe` may have functioned as a multi-category commercial / gift / promotional-goods designation under which Japanese-made products such as lighters and clocks were supplied, including corporate-branded examples.

Reasons to test, not adopt:
- exact Modern De Luxe wording exists on both clocks and lighters;
- multiple Modern De Luxe lighters carry corporate advertising or commemorative branding;
- Modern De Luxe clocks also survive as corporate commemoratives (Mitsubishi Nagoya Works; Fukuoka Prefectural Kyosairen corpus already recorded elsewhere);
- Hirota's official history independently places the company in commemorative-gift business from 1953 and later export/sales structures.

Missing bridge:
- no period source yet identifies the legal owner or supplier of `Modern De Luxe`;
- no Modern De Luxe lighter has yet been directly tied to Hirota/H.M.C. by maker marking or period company document;
- no Modern De Luxe clock has yet been directly tied to the same supplier.

## 5. Search result — direct `Modern De Luxe` → Hirota / H.M.C. bridge NOT recovered
Targeted exact-string searches were run for combinations of:
- `Modern De Luxe` + Hirota / Hirota & Co.
- `Modern De Luxe` + H.M.C. / H.M.C.CO.
- `Modern De Luxe` + Modernlite
- `Modern De Luxe` + patent / PAT. PEND. / catalogue / Tokyo / Japan

No independent period source or maker-marked object was recovered that directly equates Modern De Luxe with Hirota or H.M.C. This is a negative search result, not an absence proof.

## Current judgment
This research pass produced a **material change in search space**, not a manufacturer attribution. The strongest new fact is cross-category identity: `Modern De Luxe` is physically represented on Japanese lighter products as well as clocks, and multiple lighter examples are corporate/advertising goods. The highest-yield route is therefore now to identify the supplier/owner through the lighter and promotional-goods side, then test that identity against the clock corpus.
