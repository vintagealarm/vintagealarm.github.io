### What this changes

Before this sweep, the strongest structure was:

- clocks: `Modern De Luxe`
- lighters: `MODERN / Modernlite / H.M.C.` and Hirota-manufactured `Modern Feudor`

The new surviving-object corpus narrows that gap: the **exact `Modern De Luxe` designation also exists on Japanese lighters**. This makes `Modern De Luxe` a cross-product designation spanning at least clocks and smoking-accessory/advertising products in the surviving corpus.

This is material progress because the investigation no longer has to bridge from a generic `Modern` prefix alone.

### What this does NOT prove

- It does not prove Hirota owned `Modern De Luxe`.
- It does not prove H.M.C. used `Modern De Luxe`.
- It does not prove the clock and lighter were manufactured by the same factory.
- It does not prove a manufacture decade from seller dating.
- It does not prove Modern Royal handled these specific objects.

Corporate identity remains OPEN.

## Commerce / novelty implication — HOLD

The exact `Modern De Luxe` lighter designation appears on surviving advertising or commemorative objects associated with multiple unrelated customers/organizations (AAA Southern California, Cott Soda, Konica/Sakura Film; additional petroleum-company advertising examples surfaced but are not independently dated).

Separately, Modern De Luxe clocks survive as corporate commemoratives for Mitsubishi Electric Nagoya Works and Fukuoka Prefectural Kyosairen.

Working inference only: `Modern De Luxe` may have participated in a broader promotional-goods / corporate-gift distribution channel rather than functioning only as a conventional consumer clock marque. This is now a higher-value hypothesis because the same exact designation crosses product classes and promotional use cases.

Do not promote this to ADOPTED until a period catalogue, invoice, company record, trademark record, or supplier identification connects the commerce chain.
