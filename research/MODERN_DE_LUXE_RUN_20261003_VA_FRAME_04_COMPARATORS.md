## Purpose

Compare Modern De Luxe with maker-marked travel alarms from the same broad product class. The goal is not to assign a maker from superficial similarity, but to separate generic travel-alarm architecture from a useful platform fingerprint.

## Maker-marked Japanese comparators

### Seiko
Surviving folding travel alarms are marked Japan and use 2-jewel mechanical movements. Rear plates carry separate time/alarm controls, regulator and stop functions.

### Rhythm
Surviving folding travel alarms are marked Japan and use a different rear arrangement with multiple knobs/ring keys and alarm/stop controls.

### Tokyo Tokei
2-jewel Japan folding travel alarms survive under the Tokyo Tokei name. Same product class and jewel count do not establish a Modern De Luxe link.

### Elgin No. 8822
A late-1960s Elgin folding travel alarm is documented with original box and instruction booklet as Made in Japan. A surviving example is personalized for a 1969 corporate milestone.

Its rear control plate has separate alarm/time winding and setting controls plus a regulator. This is a useful comparator because it shows a foreign watch brand selling a Japanese-made travel alarm in the same period and because the same product class could also be used as a corporate commemorative object.

Current source:
https://thedesignersconsignment.com/product/44551

## Comparison result

The current maker-marked examples show that these traits are generic category features and not enough for maker attribution:
- 2 jewels
- Japan marking
- folding case
- rear winding/setting controls
- regulator
- alarm stop/control
- corporate personalization

No inspected Seiko, Rhythm, Tokyo Tokei or Elgin rear-control arrangement is an exact match that is strong enough to identify the Modern De Luxe base movement.

## Important commercial-production lesson

The Elgin No. 8822 comparator demonstrates a period pattern directly relevant to the unresolved Modern De Luxe company problem:

> a well-known foreign brand could sell a folding mechanical travel alarm that was physically made in Japan, with its own brand on the front and corporate personalization added to an example.

This does not identify the Modern De Luxe supplier. It does show why dial brand, manufacturing country, distributor and commemorative customer must remain separate fields.

## What would count as a real platform match

A comparator should share several low-level details, not just the product category:
- rear plate geometry
- screw locations
- control-shaft positions
- regulator position and arc
- alarm stop/release geometry
- winding-key forms
- central plate/bridge form
- movement dimensions
- maker mark on an otherwise matching movement

## Box ④ status

The comparator field is now better bounded, but the Modern De Luxe platform is still unidentified.

Do not revive Tokyo Clock or another maker from category-level similarity.
