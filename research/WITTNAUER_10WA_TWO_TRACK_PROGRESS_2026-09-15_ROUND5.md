## Track A — chronology / surviving examples

### A17. A second 1216-family case sits immediately below the dated 1959 example

EveryWatch preserves a dealer listing for a Wittnauer Ref. `1216 A`, Cal.10WA, with case number `273746`. The inside case is described as signed `Longines-Wittnauer Watch Co. Inc. New York`.

Source:
- https://everywatch.com/wittnauer/watch-12436882

This can now be compared with the EBTH Ref.1216 / serial `274320` carrying the presentation inscription dated `1959-02-10`.

Classification: `ADOPTED — surviving-case observations / HOLD — factory serial chronology`.

Research consequence:

- two independently documented 1216-family cases fall very close together at `273746` and `274320`;
- one of those examples has a dated 1959 provenance inscription;
- this strengthens the value of case-number clustering as an archival search key, but it still does not prove production sequence, batch size, or manufacturing date.

Do not convert the seller's approximate `1950` year into a factory date.

### A18. Beitl gives a specific 1956 date for a rare Longines-signed 10WA, but his collaboration explanation is explicitly inferential

`Alarm am Arm` p.295 describes a rare **LONGINES** alarm watch using `Longines/Wittnauer 10 WA` and calls the example a watch `von 1956` (from 1956). It then states that the watch is essentially a Wittnauer and says it is **to be assumed** (`Es ist anzunehmen`) that, because Wittnauer used a Longines hand-wound calibre and added its own alarm module, Longines produced a small series through that cooperation.

Classification:

- `HOLD — specialist secondary chronology lead` for the 1956 date;
- `HOLD — author inference` for the claimed Longines/Wittnauer production cooperation;
- `OPEN — primary confirmation` for both.

Important editorial separation:

> Beitl's 1956 date is useful as a search target, but the book itself signals that the collaboration explanation is an assumption, not a quoted factory fact.

Next search should target Longines catalogues / archives / dealer material around 1955–1957 for a Longines-signed alarm using 10WA.

## Track B — base calibre evidence

### B16. A mainspring interchange catalogue directly groups Wittnauer 10WA with AS 1200-family calibres

A CousinsUK-hosted GR mainspring catalogue gives White-A-Loy mainspring stock number `4486`, dimensions `1.60 × 0.10 × 300`, strength `9`, and lists in the same interchange group:

- `AS 1200 à 1202` and numerous later AS calibres;
- `Wittnauer 10 S, 10 SC, 10 SB, 10 SG, 10 WA, 11 H, 1317, 1496`;
- many other calibres sharing the same mainspring dimensions / replacement spring.

Sources:
- https://www.cousinsuk.com/pdf/skus/9931_gr%20mainsprings_compressed.pdf
- https://www.cousinsuk.com/PDF/categories/7831_GR%20Pages%20295%20-%20304.pdf

The same catalogue places common Longines `10 L / 10 LN / 10 LNS / 10 LSN / 10 N / 10 LS` families under different mainspring stock numbers (`4467` / `4479` groups depending on calibre).

Classification: `ADOPTED — direct mainspring interchange evidence / still NOT exact base-calibre proof`.

Why this matters:

- the AS1200 relationship is now visible in a complete replacement-mainspring table, not only in isolated modern seller listings;
- the 10WA entry is explicitly in the same replacement spring group as AS1200–1202;
- common Longines 10-series entries shown on the same catalogue page use other spring groups.

Why this still does not settle the base movement:

- mainsprings are dimensional interchange items and stock `4486` is shared by many unrelated movement families;
- sharing a mainspring does not prove plate architecture or ebauche identity;
- Horlbeck and Beitl still independently describe a Longines base;
- existing research also has other AS1200-family part interchanges, so the cumulative AS evidence is strengthening, but the direct manufacturer/service bridge is still missing.

Therefore `Longines base vs AS1200` remains `CONFLICT`, with the evidentiary balance increasingly worth testing against a period Wittnauer parts sheet.

### B17. Bestfit / parts-search false-positive rule is refined

The earlier warning about `WA10` remains valid: `WA10` can occur as a stock/material code unrelated to Wittnauer Cal.10WA.

However, the GR mainspring table above is **not** such a false positive: it explicitly spells out `Wittnauer ... 10 WA` inside an interchange group that also names `AS 1200 à 1202`.

Research rule:

> Require explicit `Wittnauer 10 WA` context before treating a parts-catalogue hit as evidence; once explicit context is present, record the exact part category and stock number rather than converting interchangeability into calibre identity.

## Round 5 synthesis

Three chronology / identity constraints are now sharper:

1. Ref.1216-family serials `273746` and `274320` are independently documented; the latter has a 1959-02-10 provenance inscription.
2. Beitl supplies a specialist-secondary `1956` date for a Longines-signed 10WA example, but marks the cooperation story as inference.
3. Direct mainspring interchange data add another independent AS1200-family linkage while simultaneously showing that common Longines 10-series calibres on the same page use different replacement spring groups.

None of these is sufficient alone to resolve the base-calibre conflict. Together they make a period Wittnauer / Longines service-parts sheet the decisive next target.
