## 1. Question

The research question is not “what story sounds plausible around a giant alarm watch?” It is:

> What period use contexts for portable alarm clocks, travel alarms, corporate commemorative clocks, and wristwatch-form novelty clocks can actually be documented, and which of those contexts explain Modern De Luxe without inventing an origin story?

## 2. Period-primary background: why travel alarms existed

A 1951 U.S. patent for a travel alarm clock case, published in 1953 as US2640668A, explicitly explains the problem the category was designed to solve.

It states that portable alarm clocks were used by:
- travelling salesmen
- tourists
- other travellers staying in hotels, sleeping cars, passenger ships, etc.

It further explains that:
- the clock must occupy as little luggage space as possible while travelling;
- its case therefore closes compactly for transport;
- when placed on a night table or bureau, the case can open/extend to expose and support the clock.

Source:
https://patents.google.com/patent/US2640668A/en

**Direct consequence:** folding / sliding travel-alarm cases were not merely decorative packaging. Compact transport + protected carriage + bedside self-support were explicit design requirements of the period.

This directly explains the broader product category into which the Modern De Luxe folding travel alarms fit.

## 3. Period advertising: portability and giftability were both active selling points

Westclox period advertising provides two useful independent examples.

### 1950
A Saturday Evening Post / Westclox advertisement presented the Travalarm travel alarm among “Four Thoughtful Ways to Say Best Wishes.”

Source:
https://clockhistory.com/0/document-11425-1.html

This is direct period evidence that a travel alarm could be marketed as a gift, not only as personal travel equipment.

### 1951
Another Westclox advertisement used the headline “You can take it with you!” and featured the Travalarm travel alarm alongside wrist and pocket watches.

Source:
https://clockhistory.com/0/document-11534-1.html

This directly emphasizes portability.

## 4. Category continuity into the Modern De Luxe period

Westclox's Deluxe Travette travel alarm is documented as:
- introduced 1960
- discontinued 1968
- spring powered
- folding leather-over-metal case
- usage location explicitly classified as “Travel”
- functions: time + alarm

Source:
https://clockhistory.com/0/westclox/model-numbers/44016/model-2096-1.html

The earlier Travette was introduced in 1956 and discontinued in 1959.

Source:
https://clockhistory.com/0/westclox/model-2095-1.html?page=40

**Research consequence:** through the late 1950s and 1960s, compact mechanical travel alarms were a normal commercial category rather than an isolated novelty.

This gives a period market background for the Modern De Luxe folding travel alarms without asserting a corporate relationship to Westclox.

## 5. Modern De Luxe-specific use: Japanese corporate commemorative clocks

Two Modern De Luxe examples already in the project corpus place the brand directly in corporate-commemorative use.

### Mitsubishi Electric Nagoya Works
A surviving Modern De Luxe travel alarm carries a 50th-anniversary marking for Mitsubishi Electric Nagoya Works.

Current surviving-object page:
https://noraneco1972.jimdofree.com/%E3%83%96%E3%83%AD%E3%82%B0-menu/%E6%99%82%E8%A8%88/%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%99%E3%83%AB%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E3%83%83%E3%82%AF/

The seller interprets the anniversary as 1974 from the works' founding date. Keep that dating as seller inference unless independently confirmed; the commemorative marking itself is the useful object evidence.

### Fukuoka Kyosairen
The project corpus also contains the Modern De Luxe alarm marked for Fukuoka Kyosairen's 20th-anniversary / one-trillion-yen milestone. The issuer's own twenty-year history is independently dated to 1975 in library records.

**Direct consequence:** Modern De Luxe clocks were used at least in some Japanese corporate/institutional commemorative-gift contexts.

This is a real use context, not merely a theory about distribution.

## 6. Oversized wristwatch-form object: what is and is not established

Beitl calls the 55 mm Modern du Luxe wrist alarm a curiosity and says that a standing alarm was effectively placed into a wristwatch-form case.

That confirms the unusual construction but not the commercial intention behind it.

Current marketplace records also preserve much larger Modern De Luxe wristwatch-form clocks, including wall-clock-sized examples. However, current sellers describe them with modern terms such as novelty / wall clock / office decoration.

**Boundary:** there is still no period advertisement, catalogue, or company text in the current corpus explaining whether the 55 mm wearable-form object was sold as:
- a humorous novelty,
- a serious wearable alarm,
- a promotional object,
- a travel clock with strap,
- or some combination.

Do not infer intended use from today's sellers or from Beitl's collector commentary.

## 7. Box ② result

The supported demand/use background is now:

1. **Portable travel alarm need:** period-primary patent evidence explicitly documents compact luggage transport and bedside use.
2. **Travel-alarm market:** period advertisements and model histories show the category was commercially normal in the 1950s–60s.
3. **Gift use:** period Westclox advertising demonstrates that travel alarms were marketed as gifts.
4. **Modern De Luxe-specific commemorative use:** surviving Mitsubishi and Fukuoka examples show Japanese corporate/institutional commemorative deployment.
5. **55 mm wristwatch-form purpose:** still unresolved. We know the construction, not the original marketing rationale.

This is enough to explain the broader environment without turning “novelty” into an invented historical purpose.

## 8. Stopping rule for box ②

Do not broaden into generic postwar travel or gift history unless it explains a Modern De Luxe feature.

Reopen this box when:
- a period Modern De Luxe advertisement/catalogue appears;
- original packaging states intended use;
- a corporate procurement source explains why the clock was chosen;
- a contemporary source describes the giant wristwatch-form product's target use.
