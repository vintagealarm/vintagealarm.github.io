# Modern De Luxe — 2026-09-29/30 research addendum

## Research control
This file records evidence without changing public WATCH prose or claiming a final manufacturer attribution. Evidence grades remain separated from seller dating and inference.

## 1. Modern De Luxe Mitsubishi Nagoya 50th-anniversary travel alarm — HOLD, strongest external dating anchor found so far
A surviving Japanese shop/archive page documents a mechanical `MODERN DE LUXE / ALARM CLOCK` travel alarm bearing `三菱電機株式会社 名古屋製作所50周年記念`. The object is approx. 6.7 cm diameter / 3.5 cm depth and is described as using one mainspring for time and alarm.

Source: https://noraneco1972.jimdofree.com/ブログ-menu/時計/トラベルクロック/

Dating judgment: 1924 + 50 gives 1974, but Mitsubishi primary documentation proving distribution of this exact clock in 1974 has not yet been obtained. Record as `1974 inferred from commemorative inscription`, not a primary-source-confirmed manufacture date.

## 2. Modern / 4 JEWELS / JAPAN travel alarm — ADOPTED for physical clock branding, no corporate bridge
The same source documents a book-form mechanical travel alarm marked `Modern`, `4JEWELS`, `JAPAN`. This establishes that a `Modern`-marked Japanese mechanical alarm-clock product existed. It does not by itself connect that clock to Hirota, H.M.C., Modern Royal, or Modern De Luxe.

## 3. Modern De Luxe travel-alarm corpus — ADOPTED for existence, dating weak
Surviving listings document multiple `Modern De Luxe` mechanical travel alarms, including `2 Jewels` examples attributed/marked Japan and a separate French second-hand example described as `Modern de Luxe - Japan`.

Sources:
- https://www.ebay.com/itm/165225041705
- https://www.ebay.com/itm/205293449679
- https://www.label-emmaus.co/fr/reveil-de-voyage-modern-de-luxe-japan-109797152/

These establish a repeatable product corpus, not a maker or date.

## 4. Beitl primary research source for the 55 mm wrist alarm — ADOPTED object description; date NOT supplied
Leonhard Beitl, `Alarm am Arm`, p.317 describes `MODERN DU LUXE` as a gold-plated metal case of 55 mm, silvered dial, printed black Roman numerals, luminous Dauphine hands and a 2-jewel pin-lever movement. Beitl explicitly characterizes it as essentially a table alarm inserted into a wristwatch case; the apparent crown at 3 is decorative and all controls are on the back.

Crucially, the p.317 description does **not** assign a manufacture year or decade. The book's movement table lists `Modern du Luxe` with `Kal. Modern du Luxe` rather than identifying a base movement manufacturer. Therefore Beitl cannot currently serve as a dating anchor or OEM attribution.

## 5. Seller claims of 1950s/1960s — REJECTED as dating anchors
A current/archived eBay result titles a giant Modern De Luxe wall/wristwatch-form clock as `1950s`; Pamono/Chairish/Etsy market another giant wristwatch-form alarm as `1960s`, with some sellers also claiming Germany. None of the surfaced pages supplies a dated period document, serial chronology, dated packaging, invoice, catalogue, guarantee, or other independent basis for those decade/manufacture-country claims.

Examples:
- https://www.ebay.it/itm/144955837530 (`1950s` seller title)
- https://www.pamono.eu/wristwatch-from-modern-de-luxe-1960s
- https://www.etsy.com/listing/4320333340/vintage-modern-de-luxe-oversized-1960s

Decision: do not use `1950s`, `1960s`, or `Germany` from these listings in the chronology. They remain search leads only.

## 6. Earliest-dated-clock search result as of 2026-09-30
After targeted web searches across English/German/French variants (`Modern De Luxe`, `Modern du Luxe`, alarm/travel clock/watch, 1950/1960, Japan, Hirota, H.M.C.) and re-checking the uploaded Beitl source, **no pre-1971 Modern De Luxe clock has yet been found with independent contemporaneous dating evidence**.

The strongest externally anchored surviving clock remains the Mitsubishi Nagoya Works 50th-anniversary example, provisionally tied to 1974 by its commemorative inscription. This is a negative search result, not proof that Modern De Luxe clocks began in the 1970s.

Important consequence: the often-repeated online `1960s` dating of the giant wristwatch-form object is currently unsupported by the evidence set used here. Beitl confirms the object/type but does not rescue that date.

## 7. Modern H.M.C. / Modernlite physical corpus — ADOPTED for existence, HOLD for corporate expansion
Surviving objects establish `Modern`, `Modernlite`, `H.M.C.` and Japanese lighter production as a real physical corpus. The formal expansion/legal identity of `H.M.C.` remains unresolved.

## 8. Modern Royal official lineage — chronological control
Modern Royal's official detailed history states: 1946 Ichikawa Sangyo; 1965 Royal Industries; 1971 joint venture with Hirota Modern Trading and renaming to Modern Royal.

Source: https://www.modernroyal.com/docs/enkaku.pdf

## 9. Hirota was itself a lighter producer — ADOPTED
A 1983 UNDP/UNIDO report describes `Hirota & Co., Ltd.` as specializing in producing cigarette lighters and records metalworking capabilities including moulding and plating.

Sources:
- https://www.unido.org/publications/ot/9651407/pdf
- https://downloads.unido.org/ot/48/04/4804284/10001-15000_13029.pdf

## 10. MODERN Derringer → Hirota attribution — HOLD; physical MODERN branding ADOPTED

The listing title attributes it to `Hirota & Co LTD Japan`, but no HIROTA marking is visible in the supplied screenshot. Independent Wikimedia/Flickr metadata makes the same attribution, still secondary.

## 11. 1968 Zurich Commercial Court record: Hirota manufactured `Modern Feudor` lighters — ADOPTED, major bridge
A published decision of the Handelsgericht des Kantons Zürich dated 26 July 1968 records lighters manufactured by `Hirota & Co., Ltd.` in Tokyo bearing `Feudor` or `Modern Feudor`, and describes disputed `Modern Feudor` products as Hirota production. It also records assertions/documents around a 6 March 1962 licence/manufacturing contract, a 9 July 1964 Hirota confirmation and a 9 April 1965 Hirota letter.

Source: https://www.decisions.ch/smi/1969.pdf

Direct conclusion: no later than the mid-1960s, Hirota was manufacturing a lighter line using a `Modern`-prefixed designation. This does not establish standalone `MODERN` ownership, `H.M.C. = Hirota`, or any Modern De Luxe clock connection.

## 12. OEM comparison — passive only
`2 Jewels`, shared mainspring arrangements, and mixed auction-lot co-occurrence are not sufficiently discriminating manufacturer fingerprints. Tokyo Tokei and other makers remain only in a general comparison pool unless a strong image-level/mechanical identity appears independently.

## Current working model
Confirmed/strong surrounding structure:

`Hirota & Co. — lighter manufacturer`
→ `Modern Feudor — Hirota production by mid-1960s`

Parallel physical lighter corpus:
`MODERN / Modernlite` → `H.M.C.CO., TOKYO`

Corporate succession:
`Hirota Modern Trading + Royal Industries` → `Modern Royal` (1971)

Clock corpus:
`Modern / 4 JEWELS / JAPAN`
`Modern De Luxe / Japanese mechanical travel alarms`
`Modern du Luxe / 55 mm wrist alarm (Beitl)`

The central defect remains unchanged: the clock corpus still lacks a period document naming Hirota, H.M.C., Hirota Modern Trading, or Modern Royal.

## Locked research priority after repeated drift
The immediate clock-side question is now strictly:

> **What is the earliest independently dateable Modern De Luxe clock/watch evidence?**

Acceptable dating evidence: period catalogue/advertisement, dated guarantee/invoice, corporate commemorative inscription corroborated by company history, period patent/trademark/design record, dated export directory, or other contemporaneous documentary provenance.

Do not promote seller decade estimates, generic jewel counts, style dating, mixed lots, or movement resemblance into chronology.

Next search targets:
1. period export catalogues and trade directories for `Modern De Luxe` / `Modern du Luxe` clocks;
2. boxed/deadstock examples with guarantees, instruction sheets, importer/exporter labels or dated corporate presentation inscriptions;
3. trademark/design/utility-model records specifically tied to the clock name or physical case;
4. additional corporate commemorative Modern De Luxe clocks that can supply external dates;
5. only after a dated clock is secured, compare its date/markings against the Hirota/MODERN corporate timeline.
