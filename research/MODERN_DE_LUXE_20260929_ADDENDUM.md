## 1. Modern De Luxe Mitsubishi Nagoya 50th-anniversary travel alarm — HOLD, strong dating anchor

A surviving Japanese shop/archive page documents a mechanical `MODERN DE LUXE / ALARM CLOCK` travel alarm, metal/gold-colour, coin design, approx. 6.7 cm diameter and 3.5 cm depth. The item bears the inscription `三菱電機株式会社 名古屋製作所50周年記念`.

Source:
https://noraneco1972.jimdofree.com/ブログ-menu/時計/トラベルクロック/

The seller/page dates the piece to 1974 from the Nagoya Works' 1924 establishment and describes it as a likely commemorative novelty. The same page states that the clock and alarm use a single mainspring and that the alarm functions.

**Directly supported by the page:** a surviving `MODERN DE LUXE / ALARM CLOCK` travel alarm exists with a Mitsubishi Electric Nagoya Works 50th-anniversary inscription; it is a mechanical alarm clock and the page describes a single-mainspring clock/alarm arrangement.

**Dating judgment / HOLD:** the 50th-anniversary inscription provides a materially stronger dating anchor than a marketplace style estimate. If the anniversary is counted from the Nagoya Works' 1924 establishment, the intended anniversary year is 1974. Mitsubishi primary documentation proving that this exact clock was distributed in 1974 has not yet been obtained.

## 5. Modern Royal official lineage — chronological control

Modern Royal's official detailed history states: 1946 Ichikawa Sangyo; 1965 Royal Industries; 1971 joint venture with Hirota Modern Trading and renaming to Modern Royal.

Source:
https://www.modernroyal.com/docs/enkaku.pdf

Therefore explicit `Modern Royal` branding should not be assigned to the 1960s merely from seller dating without explaining the conflict with the official company-name chronology.

## 7. Hirota was itself a lighter producer — ADOPTED

A 1983 UNDP/UNIDO report describes `Hirota & Co., Ltd.` as specializing in producing cigarette lighters and records metalworking capabilities including moulding and plating. This fixes Hirota as a manufacturing entity, not merely a trading label, by that date.

## 9. 1968 Zurich Commercial Court record: Hirota manufactured `Modern Feudor` lighters — ADOPTED, major bridge

A published decision of the Handelsgericht des Kantons Zürich dated 26 July 1968, reproduced in *Schweizerische Mitteilungen über Gewerblichen Rechtsschutz und Urheberrecht* (1969), provides period legal evidence directly connecting Hirota & Co., Ltd. in Tokyo with a `Modern`-prefixed lighter designation.

Source:
https://www.decisions.ch/smi/1969.pdf
Relevant PDF pp. 74–78 (document pagination around pp. 71–76).

The court record states that the defendants imported lighters manufactured by the Japanese company `Hirota & Co., Ltd.` in Tokyo and bearing the marks `Feudor` or `Modern Feudor`. It further describes the disputed `Modern Feudor` products as coming from Hirota's production.

The decision also records documentary assertions concerning a licence/manufacturing contract dated 6 March 1962 between Hirota and the plaintiff, a Hirota confirmation dated 9 July 1964 concerning export rights, and a Hirota letter dated 9 April 1965. The court notes that the `Feudor` mark was stamped into the bottom of the disputed lighters.

**ADOPTED direct conclusion:** no later than the mid-1960s, Hirota & Co., Ltd. in Tokyo was manufacturing cigarette lighters sold under a designation containing the word `Modern` (`Modern Feudor`). This is period legal evidence and is substantially stronger than collector/seller attribution.

**What this does NOT yet prove:**
- it does not establish that `MODERN` by itself was legally owned by Hirota;
- it does not establish that `H.M.C.` expands to Hirota;
- it does not establish that the Derringer product was manufactured by Hirota, although it makes the recurring collector attribution materially more plausible;
- it does not connect `Modern De Luxe` clocks/watches to Hirota.

**Research significance:** the previous central bridge `MODERN-family name → Hirota?` is no longer supported only by modern collector metadata. A period judicial record independently establishes Hirota production of a `Modern`-prefixed lighter line before the 1971 Modern Royal joint venture. This materially strengthens the model that a MODERN-family commercial/product vocabulary existed on the Hirota side before the Hirota Modern Trading + Royal Industries combination.

## 10. Hirota later held U.S. cigarette-lighter designs — ADOPTED as corporate capability

U.S. design-patent indexes record multiple cigarette-lighter designs filed in 1984–1985 with `Kabushiki Kaisha Hirota` as assignee and Yoshio Hirota as inventor. This supports independent Hirota lighter design/manufacturing activity but does not identify H.M.C. or Modern De Luxe.

## Current working model after 1968 court evidence

The investigation has moved materially closer to the Hirota side:

`Hirota & Co., Ltd. — lighter manufacturer (period legal evidence)`
→ `Modern Feudor — Hirota production, mid-1960s (period legal evidence)`

Parallel physical corpus:

`MODERN / Modernlite`
→ `H.M.C.CO., TOKYO` (physical marks)

Corporate succession:

`Hirota Modern Trading + Royal Industries`
→ `Modern Royal` (1971 official history)

Clock corpus:

`Modern / Modern De Luxe`
→ Japanese mechanical alarm products (physical/market corpus)

The missing bridges have narrowed. The priority is now to determine whether the `Modern` in `Modern Feudor`, the standalone `MODERN`/H.M.C. line, and `Modern De Luxe` are demonstrably part of the same Hirota-controlled brand/product family rather than merely sharing a generic word.
