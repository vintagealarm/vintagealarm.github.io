## 1. Question

For the VA standard DEEP DIVE frame, the first question is not “can Hirota be proven?” but:

> What company / production / sourcing context can currently be stated for the Modern De Luxe clocks themselves?

This pass therefore rechecked the specialist source, exact-brand clock records, current marketplace records, Project/Library holdings, and already-adopted company hypotheses. It did not use lighter-side evidence to fill a clock-side gap.

## 2. Specialist-source baseline

Leonhard Beitl, *Alarm am Arm*, p.317 describes the 55 mm `MODERN DU LUXE` wrist alarm as:

- gold-plated metal case
- silvered dial with black Roman numerals
- Dauphine luminous hands
- 2-jewel pin-lever movement
- effectively a standing/table alarm placed in a wristwatch-form case
- 3 o'clock exterior crown is a dummy
- all operating controls are on the rear

The movement table later lists:

- `Modern du Luxe — Kal. Modern du Luxe`

It does **not** name a movement manufacturer, finished-clock manufacturer, brand owner, exporter, or distributor.

**Research consequence:** the specialist literature establishes the object and its architecture, but supplies no company identity.

### German attribution conflict

Pamono currently lists an oversized wristwatch-form Modern De Luxe alarm clock and assigns:
- manufacturer: `Modern De Luxe`
- country of manufacture: Germany
- production period: 1960s

Source:
https://www.pamono.eu/wristwatch-from-modern-de-luxe-1960s

An Etsy listing makes the same Germany-manufacture claim for an oversized wristwatch-form Modern De Luxe alarm:
https://www.etsy.com/listing/4320333340/vintage-modern-de-luxe-oversized-1960s

These are seller/platform attributions, not contemporaneous manufacturer records.

**Boundary:** do not convert this into “Modern De Luxe was a German company.” The current corpus contains Japan-marked/Japan-attributed clock records and Germany-attributed giant wristwatch-form clocks without a primary source that reconciles them.

These objects show that Modern De Luxe clocks entered Japanese corporate-gift / commemorative channels, but neither surviving example currently names the supplier, distributor, or maker.

**Research consequence:** a Japanese commercial supply channel is evidenced at the product/provenance level, but the responsible company remains open.

## 6. What box ① can now safely say

For a future DEEP DIVE, the company section can currently support:

1. `Modern De Luxe / Modern du Luxe` is securely evidenced as a clock name/brand marking in specialist literature and surviving clocks.
2. Specialist literature does not identify the underlying movement or finished-clock manufacturer.
3. A Japanese clock/commercial channel is real: Japan-marked/Japan-attributed travel alarms and Japanese corporate commemorative examples survive.
4. Separate marketplace records attribute some giant wristwatch-form Modern De Luxe clocks to Germany, but that attribution is not primary evidence and conflicts with the Japanese clock corpus.
5. No current source proves that Hirota, H.M.C., Modern Royal, Tokyo Clock, or a legal entity named Modern De Luxe manufactured or commercially operated the clock line.

That is enough to **bound the company context without inventing a company**.
