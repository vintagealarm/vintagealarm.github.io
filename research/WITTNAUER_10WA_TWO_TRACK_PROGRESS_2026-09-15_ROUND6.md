## Track A — Wittnauer adoption / product-history

### A17. Beitl gives a much tighter specialist chronology than the previous generic `1950s` dating

A targeted re-read of *Alarm am Arm* produced two separate statements that must not be collapsed into one.

On the Wittnauer pages Beitl states that the first Wittnauer alarm model using Cal.10WA was produced in the **first half of the 1950s** (`in der ersten Hälfte der Fünfzigerjahre`). He then shows an AS 1475-powered Wittnauer and dates that example to **1955**, calling it one of the first models fitted with AS calibres (`gehört zu den ersten Modellen, die mit AS-Kalibern ausgerüstet wurden`).

Classification: `ADOPTED — specialist-book chronology claim / HOLD — factory chronology`.

Research consequence:

- the working question is no longer only `did Wittnauer later switch to AS?`;
- Beitl specifically places early Wittnauer AS adoption **by 1955**;
- therefore 10WA development / launch most plausibly belongs before or around that transition, not in a vague late-1950s window;
- this makes a dated 1953–1955 Wittnauer price list / bulletin particularly valuable.

This is not yet primary factory evidence. Do not convert Beitl's 1955 specimen date into a guaranteed first-AS-adoption date.

Source: uploaded `Alarm Am Arm (1).pdf`, Wittnauer section around printed pp. 499–500.

### A18. Beitl separately records a Longines-signed 10WA example as 1956 — but his explanation of why Longines made it is explicitly conjectural

In the Longines section Beitl describes a Longines-branded watch with `Kaliber Longines/Wittnauer 10 WA` and calls it a very rare watch from **1956**.

The next sentence matters editorially. Beitl writes `Es ist anzunehmen, dass durch diese Zusammenarbeit Longines eine kleine, eigene Serie dieser Uhr fertigte.` The phrase `Es ist anzunehmen` marks the proposed explanation — that cooperation led Longines to make a small series — as the author's inference, not a quoted factory fact.

Classification:

- `ADOPTED — Beitl specialist dating of the illustrated Longines-signed 10WA to 1956`;
- `HOLD — explanation that cooperation caused a small Longines production series`.

Research consequence:

- 10WA-type production / branding may have overlapped with Wittnauer's early AS era rather than ending cleanly before 1955;
- alternatively the Longines-signed example may be a separate or later small run;
- only dated factory / dealer material can resolve whether 1955 represents an abrupt replacement, an overlap, or parallel cataloguing.

Source: uploaded `Alarm Am Arm (1).pdf`, Longines section printed p. 295.

## Track B — patent / CH304088A connection history

### B16. Re-check of CH304088A confirms that the production weak point was already anticipated in the patent concept

The patent text expressly states that the rotating bezel can simultaneously wind the alarm barrel and drive alarm-time setting. It then says that fitting the alarm barrel with a **slipping bridle** (`ressort à bride glissante`) would preserve complete freedom to modify the alarm setting even when the spring is fully wound.

Source:
- https://patents.google.com/patent/CH304088A/en

Classification: `ADOPTED patent-content fact / OPEN production implementation`.

This strengthens the technical question:

> If production 10WA reaches a hard full-wind limit that can lead to transmission-tooth damage, what exact production barrel / transmission arrangement replaced or omitted the patent's slipping-bridle option?

Do not infer cost-cutting, simplification, patent avoidance or supplier constraints without a service / production source.

## Round 6 synthesis

The strongest new result is chronological rather than legal.

Beitl's catalogue pages now give a specialist-source sequence in which:

1. the 10WA is described as Wittnauer's first alarm model and as produced in the **first half of the 1950s**;
2. a Wittnauer AS1475 example is dated **1955** and described as one of the first AS-powered Wittnauer alarms;
3. a rare Longines-signed 10WA is separately dated **1956**, with the proposed reason for Longines production explicitly framed by Beitl as an assumption.

This makes a simple story — `10WA ended, then AS began` — unsafe. The evidence is now more compatible with **transition / overlap / small parallel run**, pending primary sources.

On the base movement, AS1200-family interchange evidence is now broader across several part types, but the specialist books' Longines-base attribution remains unresolved. `CONFLICT` is retained.

On the patent side, the research has hit a genuine source-access boundary: the Swiss IPI's free raw register data do not cover 1952 filings. The next meaningful advance requires historical-register / archival data, not more repetition of Google-family searches.
